Post 2 (Wed, Nov 4): Your statement is a values statement

Your bank statement is a values statement.

Not what you say matters to you. What you actually fund.

Try this tonight. Pull your last 90 days of spending and sort every line into three columns:

1. Protects the people I love
2. Builds the future I want
3. I would not miss it

Most people find column three is bigger than they expected, and column one is smaller than they thought.

That is not a failure. It is information.

You cannot realign what you will not look at. Look first. Then decide.

Post 3 (Thu, Nov 5): The question most families cannot answer

If your income stopped tomorrow, how many months would your family's life stay the same?

Not roughly. In months.

Most people I ask cannot answer it. Not because they are careless, but because nobody ever asked them to do the math.

Three numbers get you there:

1. What your household costs each month
2. What you have set aside that you can reach quickly
3. What coverage you have, and whether it stays with you if you leave your job

That third one surprises people. Coverage through work is often tied to the job.

Know your number. Then you can make a real decision about it.

Post 6 (Thu, Nov 12): Debt is a math problem with an emotional cost

Debt is not a character flaw.

It is a math problem with an emotional cost.

There are two common ways people pay it down:

Smallest balance first. You clear accounts faster and build momentum.
Highest interest first. You usually pay less over time.

People argue about which one is right. Here is what I have learned from coaching: the right plan is the one you will still be following in month six.

Pick one. Write it down. Make one extra payment this month.

That is one percent better. It adds up.

Post 8 (Wed, Nov 18): One better money decision a week

You do not need a new financial life.

You need one better decision a week.

Here is a month of them:

Week 1: Write down what your household costs each month.
Week 2: Automate one transfer to savings, even a small one.
Week 3: Check who is listed as beneficiary on every account and policy you have.
Week 4: Find out whether your coverage follows you if you leave your job.

Four decisions. About an hour in total.

Small, consistent, intentional. That is how lasting change actually happens.

Post 9 (Thu, Nov 19): The form that decides everything

One of the most important financial documents you own is a form most people filled out once and forgot.

The beneficiary designation.

It decides who receives an account or a policy. In many cases it is followed even when a will says something different.

Marriage. Divorce. A new child. A loss in the family. Every one of those is a reason to look again.

Take ten minutes this week. Log in, check every account and policy, and confirm the names still match your life.

Love that is not on paper can be hard to carry out.

Most financial stress comes from thinking in months.

The bill due Friday is real. So is the person you will be at 65, and the kids who will need help with school before you feel ready.

The earlier you plan for the long run, the less each step has to carry.

So here is the question for this week: what is one thing you are doing today that your future self will thank you for?

If you cannot name one, start there.
