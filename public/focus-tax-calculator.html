How this is calculated
  • Interruptions. 23 minutes to regain deep focus after each one, five workdays a week. Phase One: Why Structure Outperforms Motivation.
  • Task switching. 20 minutes lost each time you move between fundamentally different kinds of work. Phase Twelve: The Power of Batching.
  • Meetings. 37% of meeting hours, the share attendees consider unnecessary. Phase Thirteen: How to Handle Meetings Without Letting Them Run Your Life.
  • Drift. Five hours a week if you rarely plan, two and a half if you sometimes do, one if you plan every week. Phase Ten: The Weekly Audit.
  • Your hour. Yearly earnings divided by your weekly hours across fifty working weeks. Team mode uses the average salary for every person.
  • Caps. Refocusing and switching together never count for more than 40% of a workday, and the whole tax never more than half your week, so heavy inputs cannot stack past what a real day holds.
  • The system. Two communication windows (two interruptions a day), batching (two switches), a thirty-minute meeting default with no meetings that lack an outcome, and fifteen minutes of weekly planning. Phases Six, Twelve and Thirteen.

An estimate built from your own inputs and the research cited in the book, not a guarantee.
