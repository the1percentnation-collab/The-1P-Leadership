# 1P-CLC Recorded Session Rubric: Admin Scoring Guide

The scoring guide for the recorded coaching session required for 1P Certified
Life Coach certification. This document is for reviewers. The same four
criteria and the same 2, 3 and 5 descriptors are published to students in
Module 8 (`scripts/clc-content/module-08.js`), word for word. If a descriptor
changes here, change it there in the same commit. Students and reviewers must
be reading the same standard.

Brand voice rules live in the one-percent-context skill. No em dashes.

---

## What is being scored

One full coaching session, thirty minutes or longer, submitted by the student
with written consent from the practice client. Scored 0 to 5 on four criteria,
entered through the admin review UI, which writes `presence`, `questions`,
`structure` and `nonAdvising` to the student's capstone record along with a
decision of `approved` or `revise` and written feedback.

The anchors below describe what a 2, a 3 and a 5 sound like. A 4 sits between
the 3 and the 5. A 1 sits below the 2. A 0 means the criterion was not
demonstrated at all.

---

## Scoring procedure

1. **Check the submission first.** Audio is usable, the session is continuous
   rather than edited, it runs thirty minutes or longer, and consent is on
   file. If any of those fail, return it before you spend time scoring.
2. **Watch or listen once, straight through, without scoring.** No notes on the
   rubric, no numbers. You are looking for the shape of the session and whether
   the client moved. Scoring on a first pass turns the review into a hunt for
   errors and misses the arc.
3. **Second pass: score.** Work criterion by criterion. For each one, write the
   number and one piece of evidence with a timestamp. If you cannot point to
   evidence, you do not have a score yet.
4. **Estimate coach talk time.** A rough percentage is enough. It informs
   Presence, Question Quality and Non-Advising, and it is an automatic revise
   trigger when the coach clearly dominates.
5. **Apply the pass rule**, then write the feedback before you enter the
   decision. Feedback written after the decision tends to justify the number
   instead of teaching the coach.
6. **Enter scores, feedback and decision together.** One review, one record.

Target about ninety minutes per review for a thirty to forty minute session.

## Pass rule (proposed, for Anthony to confirm)

**Proposal: a minimum of 3 on every criterion, and a total of 14 or more out of
20.**

The reasoning. The floor of 3 on every criterion prevents a coach from passing
on strength in three areas while advising through the session or never
establishing a goal. The total of 14 asks for more than four bare threes, so a
pass means competent across the board with real strength somewhere.

Anything below either bar is a `revise`, not a fail. The student resubmits a
new session with no limit on resubmissions.

Open for Anthony: whether 14 is the right total, and whether a single 2 on
Non-Advising specifically should be an automatic revise regardless of total.

## The four criteria

### 1. Presence and Listening

**A 2 sounds like:** The coach hears content and responds to content. Attention is on their own next question rather than on the client. The coach interrupts, overlaps, or moves past an emotional shift as though it did not happen.

> **Client:** I took the promotion. It has been fine. My wife has not said much about it.
>
> **Coach:** Good. So back to the delegation goal from last time. Where did you land on that?

**A 3 sounds like:** The coach tracks meaning as well as words, reflects back accurately, and lets the client finish. Some of what sits under the words is missed, but nothing important is run over.

> **Client:** I took the promotion. It has been fine. My wife has not said much about it.
>
> **Coach:** So the promotion is going fine, and something at home is less settled. Which do you want to use our time on?

**A 5 sounds like:** The coach hears what is not being said and names it cleanly, without interpreting it for the client. Silence is used deliberately. The client's own words are returned to them, and the coach stays with the client through discomfort instead of easing it.

> **Client:** I took the promotion. It has been fine. My wife has not said much about it.
>
> **Coach:** (pause) You said that last part more quietly than the rest.
>
> **Client:** Did I. She told me I would say yes before I did. She was right, and I hate that she was right.


### 2. Question Quality

**A 2 sounds like:** Questions are long, stacked, closed, or leading. Many gather information the coach already has, or deliver advice with a question mark on the end. The coach's theory drives the questions rather than the client's material.

> **Coach:** Have you thought about maybe setting a boundary there, or is that something you have already tried and it did not really work for you?

**A 3 sounds like:** Questions are mostly short, open, and asked one at a time. They follow the client rather than the coach's theory. A few slip into leading or into gathering information, and the coach recovers.

> **Coach:** What is happening in that relationship right now?
>
> **Client:** We are polite. That is about it.
>
> **Coach:** What is underneath the politeness?

**A 5 sounds like:** Questions are short, open, and pointed at ground the client has not examined. They are built from the client's own language, asked one at a time, and followed by silence. At least one question visibly changes the direction of the session.

> **Coach:** What are you protecting by staying this busy?
>
> **Client:** (long pause) I have never thought about it that way. Being needed, I think. If I am not needed I do not know who I am to them.


### 3. Session Structure

**A 2 sounds like:** No session goal is established. The conversation moves from topic to topic and ends because the clock ended. No insight is named and no action is committed to.

> **Coach:** So how have things been since last time?
>
> **Client:** Busy. Work mostly. And my brother.
>
> (The session runs forty more minutes across four subjects and closes with see you next week.)

**A 3 sounds like:** A session goal is established early and the session mostly stays with it. Time is managed. An action is named at the end, though the measure or the date may be loose.

> **Coach:** What do you want to walk out of here with today?
>
> **Client:** A decision about the move.
>
> **Coach:** So what will you do this week?
>
> **Client:** Talk to my wife about it.

**A 5 sounds like:** The full spine is present: intention, session goal, explore, insight, action, accountability. The goal is agreed in the client's own words and returned to when the conversation drifts. The client names the insight, the client proposes the action, and the action has a measure and a date before the session ends.

> **Coach:** Say back what landed for you.
>
> **Client:** That I have been waiting on permission that is not coming.
>
> **Coach:** What do you want to do with that this week?
>
> **Client:** Tell my wife Thursday night that I am taking the offer.
>
> **Coach:** How will you know it happened?
>
> **Client:** I will message you Friday morning either way.


### 4. Non-Advising

**A 2 sounds like:** The coach solves. Recommendations, suggestions, and personal stories offered as templates. The client leaves with the coach's plan rather than their own.

> **Client:** I do not know how to bring it up with her.
>
> **Coach:** What worked for me was writing it out first and picking a calm evening. Try Sunday. Definitely do not do it right after work.

**A 3 sounds like:** The coach mostly stays out of the client's answer. One or two suggestions appear, usually framed as options, and the coach hands the work back to the client afterward.

> **Client:** I do not know how to bring it up with her.
>
> **Coach:** Some people write it down first, some just say it. What fits you?
>
> **Client:** Writing it, probably.
>
> **Coach:** What would you write first?

**A 5 sounds like:** The coach stays out of the client's answer entirely, including when the client asks directly for advice. Expertise shows up as better questions, not as content. Any option offered is genuinely optional and the choice stays with the client.

> **Client:** What would you do?
>
> **Coach:** I will tell you at the end if you still want it. First, what do you already know you are going to do?
>
> **Client:** (pause) Tell her Thursday.

## Automatic revise triggers

Any one of these returns the submission as `revise` regardless of the other
scores. Name the trigger explicitly in the feedback so the student knows this
was not a close call on points.

- **Advising through the whole session.** The coach solves rather than coaches
  from start to finish. The client leaves with the coach's plan.
- **No session goal established.** The session never agrees on what it is for.
- **Coach talk time clearly dominant.** The coach is doing most of the talking.
- **Breach of confidentiality on the recording.** A third party is named or
  described in a way that identifies them, or the client's own identifying
  detail is handled carelessly. Return it and tell the student to delete the
  file.

Also return without scoring, with a short note rather than full feedback, if
the audio is unusable, the session is under thirty minutes, the recording is
edited or is a highlight reel, or consent is missing.

## How to write feedback a student can act on

Feedback is the product of the review. The number tells them where they stand.
The feedback is what makes the next session better.

Write it in this shape, six to ten sentences total:

1. **One sentence on what worked**, with the evidence. Specific, not general
   encouragement.
2. **One paragraph per criterion scored below the pass line**, each containing:
   what you heard, the timestamp, and the concrete alternative. The alternative
   is the part students act on.
3. **One closing instruction**: the single thing to change in the next session.
   One. Three changes at once produce none.

Rules for the writing:

- Quote the student back to themselves. "At 14:20 you asked whether she had
  tried talking to him" lands. "Your questions were sometimes leading" does not.
- Give the replacement, not just the diagnosis. Write the better question out in
  full so the student can hear the difference.
- Score the session, never the person. "This session advised" is accurate.
  "You are an advisor, not a coach" is not feedback, it is a verdict.
- No praise sandwiches. Students discount the opening compliment and brace for
  the middle. Say what worked because it worked, then say what to change.
- Keep guilt out of it entirely. Accountability is honest measurement plus
  belief. That applies to reviewing coaches as much as to coaching clients.
- On a `revise`, say plainly that a revise is a normal step and that there is no
  limit on resubmissions, and tell them to record a new session rather than a
  second take of the same conversation.

## Feedback-only reviews (after modules 4 and 6)

Students submit two short practice recordings before the graded capstone: one
after Module 4 and one after Module 6, fifteen to twenty minutes each, with the
same written client consent. The capstone is never a student's first reviewed
recording.

How these differ from the capstone review:

- **Same four criteria.** Presence and Listening, Question Quality, Session
  Structure, Non-Advising, read against the same 2, 3 and 5 anchors above.
- **No score is recorded.** Do not enter numbers, and do not put a number in the
  written feedback. A number this early gets treated as a verdict and students
  start coaching the rubric instead of the client.
- **Feedback names one strength and one specific change per criterion.** Eight
  items, each tied to something you actually heard. The change item is the one
  that matters, so make it concrete enough to practice this week.
- **No pass or fail, and no revise decision.** The only exception is a
  confidentiality breach or missing consent, which is addressed directly with
  the student whatever the review is for.
- **Keep the two reviews consistent.** At the Module 6 review, read your own
  Module 4 feedback for that student first and say plainly what has changed and
  what has repeated. The repeats are what the student takes into the capstone.

Target about thirty minutes per feedback-only review.
