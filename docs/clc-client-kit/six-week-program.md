# The A.L.I.G.N. Six-Week Program

A coach's delivery guide.

## What this program is

Six sessions. One A.L.I.G.N. stage per session, with the sixth session used for
integration and the forward plan. Every session runs 45 to 60 minutes and
follows the same spine: intention, session goal, explore, insight, action,
accountability.

The client you are serving is usually successful on paper and misaligned in
practice. They have the title, the income, the responsibility, and a life that
looks correct from the outside. What they do not have is agreement between that
life and what actually matters to them. Six weeks is enough to find the gap,
name what is holding it open, and build a path the client can walk without you.

Your job across all six sessions is the same. Hold the structure, ask the
question, stay out of the answer. The framework does the heavy lifting. You do
not need to be impressive. You need to be present, clear, and hard to
distract.

## Before session one

Send these three things at least three days before the first session:

1. The coaching agreement, signed before coaching begins.
2. The client workbook.
3. The Alignment Assessment, with instructions to complete it and bring the
   scores to session one. Tell them to answer quickly and honestly rather than
   carefully.

Confirm logistics in writing: the six dates, the session length, the channel,
and what happens between sessions. Confirm what confidentiality covers and
where its limits are.

## How to read the run of sheet

Each session below is written for a 55-minute frame. Two adjustments:

- **45 minutes.** Cut five minutes from explore and three from the open. Do not
  cut accountability. A session that ends without a commitment is a
  conversation.
- **60 minutes.** Add the time to explore. Never add it to your own talking.

Timings are a shape, not a stopwatch. If insight arrives at minute 20, move to
action at minute 20.

## Session 1. Awareness

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** Awareness. Seeing the belief clearly.

**Session goal:** The client leaves able to say, in one sentence, the gap
between who they are and how they currently operate, and to name one belief
that keeps the gap open.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:05 | Intention | Welcome, how the six weeks work, what you will and will not do. Ask what they are bringing into the room today. |
| 0:05 to 0:12 | Session goal | Debrief the Alignment Assessment. Scores first, reaction second. Set the session goal out loud. |
| 0:12 to 0:35 | Explore | The gap. Where life looks right and feels wrong. What they have told themselves about why. |
| 0:35 to 0:43 | Insight | Name the belief. Get it into one sentence in the client's own words. |
| 0:43 to 0:50 | Action | The Gap Statement and the Belief Sentence go into the workbook, written by the client, not by you. |
| 0:50 to 0:55 | Accountability | Confirm the workbook piece, the date of session two, and what they will notice this week. |

### Assessment debrief

Do the debrief before the exploration, not after. It gives the client a place to
start that is not a complaint. Read the two lowest stages and the one highest
stage out loud. Then ask what surprised them. Resist interpreting the scores for
them. The assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis.

### Key questions

1. What made now the moment you decided to do this?
2. If nothing changes over the next two years, what does that cost you?
3. Where does your life look right from the outside and feel wrong from the inside?
4. What did the assessment show you that you already knew?
5. What did it show you that you did not expect?
6. When you imagine the version of you that is fully aligned, what is that person doing differently on a Tuesday?
7. What have you been telling yourself about why the gap is still there?
8. Where did that explanation come from?
9. What is the sentence about yourself that you have stopped questioning?
10. What would have to be true for that sentence to be wrong?

### Workbook between sessions

**Session 1 workbook: The Gap and the Sentence.** The client writes their gap
statement, their belief sentence, traces where the sentence came from, and runs
a seven-day alignment log: one line per day on where their time went and whether
it matched what they say matters.

### What done looks like

The client can say the gap in one sentence without editing it. The belief
sentence is written in their voice, not in coaching language. They left with
something they had not said out loud before. If the session ended with a plan
instead of a sentence, you moved too early.

## Session 2. Leadership

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** Leadership. Taking ownership of your story.

**Session goal:** The client moves from explanation to ownership on the one area
they most want to change, and names the part of the pattern that is theirs.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:04 | Intention | Check in. What has shifted since last week, in a sentence. |
| 0:04 to 0:10 | Session goal | Review the alignment log. Pick the one pattern worth the hour and set it as the goal. |
| 0:10 to 0:32 | Explore | The story they tell about this pattern. Who and what is cast in it. Where the ownership line actually sits. |
| 0:32 to 0:42 | Insight | Separate the circumstance from the choice. Name what is theirs. |
| 0:42 to 0:50 | Action | The Ownership Rewrite: the same story, told with the client as the author. |
| 0:50 to 0:55 | Accountability | Confirm the workbook piece and one small act of ownership this week. |

### Key questions

1. Tell me the story of this pattern the way you usually tell it.
2. Who is the main character in that version?
3. What part of this is genuinely outside your control?
4. And what part is yours?
5. What have you been waiting for someone else to do first?
6. What does holding on to that explanation protect you from?
7. If you accepted full ownership of this tomorrow, what would you stop doing?
8. What would you start?
9. Where in your life have you already taken ownership of something hard? What did that take?
10. What is the first decision that is fully yours to make this week?

### Workbook between sessions

**Session 2 workbook: The Ownership Rewrite.** The client writes the old story
and the owned story side by side, completes the control audit table, and takes
one ownership action, then writes what it cost and what it changed.

### What done looks like

The client says something like "I have been letting that happen" without
flinching and without self-punishment. Ownership is not blame. If the session
produced guilt, back up. Guilt is the sign you pushed rather than asked.

## Session 3. Identity

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** Identity. Rewriting who you believe you are.

**Session goal:** The client names the identity underneath the pattern, tests
it against real evidence, and writes a truer sentence they are willing to live
from.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:04 | Intention | Check in on the ownership action. Short. Do not let this become the session. |
| 0:04 to 0:09 | Session goal | Return to the belief sentence from session one. Set the goal: test it and write a truer one. |
| 0:09 to 0:22 | Explore | Where the belief came from, what it has cost, and what it has protected. |
| 0:22 to 0:34 | Explore | Values excavation. What they say matters against where their calendar and money actually go. |
| 0:34 to 0:43 | Insight | The contradiction, named plainly. |
| 0:43 to 0:50 | Action | The client drafts the new identity sentence. Present tense, plain words, theirs. |
| 0:50 to 0:55 | Accountability | Confirm the workbook piece and where they will live from the new sentence this week. |

### Key questions

1. Finish this sentence honestly: I am not the kind of person who...
2. How old were you when you first believed that?
3. What has believing it cost you in the last five years?
4. What has it protected you from?
5. Name three things that genuinely matter to you, in your own words.
6. Where does your calendar contradict that list?
7. Where does your money contradict it?
8. What is one time in your life you acted against that belief and it went fine?
9. What would a truer sentence about you sound like?
10. What becomes possible if that sentence is the one you operate from?
11. Who in your life will notice first?

### Workbook between sessions

**Session 3 workbook: Values and the Truer Sentence.** The client completes the
values excavation table, logs three pieces of counter-evidence to the old
belief, writes the new identity sentence, and records one decision they made
from it during the week.

### What done looks like

The new sentence is specific and unglamorous. "I finish what I start when I
choose it on purpose" holds. "I am unstoppable" does not. The client should be
slightly uncomfortable saying it out loud. That discomfort is the work landing.

---

## Session 4. Growth

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** Growth. Choosing expansion over comfort.

**Session goal:** The client builds a full goal stack of outcome, identity, and
process, anchored to the new identity sentence.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:04 | Intention | Check in. How did the new sentence hold up under pressure? |
| 0:04 to 0:09 | Session goal | Set the goal: turn the new identity into something they can act on daily. |
| 0:09 to 0:25 | Explore | What expansion actually looks like here, and what comfort they are trading. |
| 0:25 to 0:36 | Insight | The one outcome that would matter most in ninety days, and why. |
| 0:36 to 0:48 | Action | Build the goal stack: outcome, identity, process. The client writes it. |
| 0:48 to 0:55 | Accountability | Confirm the daily process commitment and the workbook piece. |

### Key questions

1. What would you go after if you already believed the new sentence?
2. Ninety days from now, what single result would tell you something real changed?
3. How would you measure it, honestly?
4. Who do you have to be to produce that result?
5. What does that person do on an ordinary day?
6. What is the smallest daily action that proves that identity?
7. What comfort are you giving up to do this?
8. Where do you usually stop, and what is happening right at that point?
9. What is the one percent version of this: the smallest step you could repeat every day without heroics?
10. What support do you need to make this survive a bad week?

### Workbook between sessions

**Session 4 workbook: The Goal Stack.** The client completes the three-layer
stack, defines their measurement, and runs the daily process action for seven
days with a simple did or did not log.

### What done looks like

One outcome goal, one identity goal, and one process action small enough to do
on the worst day of the week. If the process action needs a good mood to
survive, it is too big. Send them back to shrink it.

---

## Session 5. Navigation

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** Navigation. Executing the path forward.

**Session goal:** The client leaves with a real execution system: friction
removed, cadence set, and a plan for what happens when they miss.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:04 | Intention | Check in on the seven-day log. Data first, story second. |
| 0:04 to 0:09 | Session goal | Set the goal: turn the plan into a path that survives a normal week. |
| 0:09 to 0:28 | Explore | Where the week actually broke. Friction, constraints, and the honest reason. |
| 0:28 to 0:38 | Insight | The structural difference between what held and what dropped. |
| 0:38 to 0:48 | Action | Design the system: triggers, environment, cadence, and the recovery rule. |
| 0:48 to 0:55 | Accountability | Confirm the cadence and the workbook piece. Flag that session six includes the reassessment. |

### Key questions

1. Where exactly did the week break?
2. What happened in the ten minutes before it broke?
3. What made the days that worked easier than the days that did not?
4. What has to be true about your environment for this to be automatic?
5. What are you saying yes to that makes this impossible?
6. What is the honest smallest version you would still do on your worst day?
7. When you miss, what is your rule for the next day?
8. Who needs to know about this so it is not carried alone?
9. What will you check weekly to know if you are still on the path?
10. What is likely to knock this off course in the next month, and what is your plan for it?

### Workbook between sessions

**Session 5 workbook: The Path.** The client completes the friction audit, sets
their weekly review questions, writes their recovery rule, and runs the full
system for a week.

### What done looks like

The client can describe what they will do, when, where, and what they will do
after a miss. The recovery rule is written down. If the plan depends on
motivation, it is not a system yet.

---

## Session 6. Integration and the forward plan

**A.L.I.G.N. focus:** The whole arc, with the client holding the pen.

**Session goal:** The client sees the movement across six weeks in their own
data, and leaves with a ninety-day plan they own without you.

### Run of sheet

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:04 | Intention | Check in. What is different about how they walked into this session? |
| 0:04 to 0:14 | Session goal | Retake and score the Alignment Assessment in session. Compare to session one, stage by stage. |
| 0:14 to 0:30 | Explore | What moved, what did not, and what they now understand about why. |
| 0:30 to 0:40 | Insight | The one sentence they are taking with them, in their words. |
| 0:40 to 0:50 | Action | Build the ninety-day forward plan: one outcome, the identity sentence, the daily process, the weekly review. |
| 0:50 to 0:55 | Accountability | Self-accountability structure, their own check-in date, and a clear close. Name what continued work would look like, once, without pressure. |

### Key questions

1. Looking at the two score sets, what moved most?
2. What stayed flat, and what do you make of that?
3. What do you know now that you did not know six weeks ago?
4. What did you stop doing?
5. What is the sentence you are taking with you?
6. What is the next ninety days about?
7. What is the one thing that, if it slipped, would undo this?
8. How will you catch that early?
9. Who holds you to this now?
10. What do you want to say to the version of yourself who sat down in session one?

### Workbook between sessions

There is no next session. The workbook's closing pages are the ninety-day plan
and the self-review page, completed in session and kept by the client.

### What done looks like

The client leaves with a written plan, a measured comparison, and the language
to run A.L.I.G.N. on the next problem without you in the room. A client who
still needs you to make progress means the program did not finish its job.

## Practitioner notes

- **Stay in scope.** Coaching is not therapy, advice, mentoring, or consulting.
  If clinical material surfaces, refer out with care and say plainly why. You
  can keep coaching the goals you set, if the client wants both.
- **Do not rescue.** Silence after a strong question is the client working. Let
  it run.
- **Accountability is honest measurement plus belief.** Never guilt. A missed
  week is information about the system, not about the person.
- **Keep the stages in order.** The most common delivery failure is jumping to
  Navigation in week two because the client asked for a plan. Plans built before
  Awareness do not hold.
- **Write nothing for the client.** They hold the pen in every workbook piece.
  Ownership is the product.

## How to sell this program

### Who it is for

People who are doing well and know something is off. Title, income, and
responsibility are in place. Alignment is not. They are not in crisis and they
are not looking to be fixed. They are tired of running a life that does not
match what they actually want, and they are ready to look at it directly.

It is not for someone in active clinical distress, someone who wants you to make
their decisions, or someone who wants a strategy consultant for their business.
Saying that clearly on a discovery call earns more trust than any pitch.

### One paragraph a client would understand

Over six weeks we work through five stages. First you see the gap between who
you are and how you are actually living, and the belief that keeps it open. Then
you take ownership of the part that is yours. Then you rewrite the sentence
about yourself that has been running the show. Then you set goals that match the
person you are becoming, not the one you are leaving. Then you build a system
that survives a normal week. You start with an assessment and you retake it at
the end, so you see the movement instead of taking my word for it. You leave
with a written ninety-day plan you can run without me.

### How to price it

Do not pick a number because it sounds reasonable. Build it.

1. **Count your delivery hours.** Six sessions, plus preparation and notes, plus
   between-session contact. Most practitioners land near twelve hours per
   client for this program.
2. **Set the income you want from coaching and the number of clients you can
   hold well.** Divide. That gives you a floor per client, not a price.
3. **Weigh the value on the client's side.** What does the misalignment cost
   them over two years, in money, health, and relationships? Your price should
   be a fraction of that cost, and it should be an easy fraction to defend out
   loud.
4. **Check the market you actually sell into.** Know what structured six-week
   engagements go for with your kind of client. Sit where your delivery and your
   proof place you. Do not sit at the bottom to be safe.
5. **Test it by saying it out loud.** If you cannot say your number without
   softening it, the problem is usually clarity about the outcome, not the
   number. Fix the clarity first.
6. **Raise it deliberately.** Set a review point: a number of completed clients
   or a date. Raise on that schedule, not on how a given call feels.

Offer one price and one payment plan, not a menu. Do not discount to close a
hesitant buyer. A discount answers a question the client did not ask and
teaches them that the price was never real.
