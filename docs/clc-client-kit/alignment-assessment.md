# The Alignment Assessment

A reflection tool for the A.L.I.G.N. program.

## What this is, and what it is not

This is a structured reflection tool. It is not a validated psychometric
instrument, it is not a diagnosis, and it does not measure your worth, your
ability, or your mental health. It is a mirror. It asks you twenty five
questions you probably already know the answers to, and it puts those answers in
one place so you can see them.

You take it once before your first session and once again in your final session.
The comparison is the point.

---

## Instructions for the client

Read each statement and score how true it is for you right now, not how true you
want it to be and not how true it was last year.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Almost never true of me |
| 2 | Rarely true of me |
| 3 | Sometimes true of me |
| 4 | Often true of me |
| 5 | Almost always true of me |

Three rules:

1. Answer quickly. Your first number is usually the honest one.
2. Do not skip. If a statement does not seem to apply, score it for the closest
   part of your life that it does touch.
3. Nobody sees this but you and your coach. An accurate low score is worth more
   than a generous high one.

Allow ten minutes. Write the date at the top.

## Scoring key

Add the five scores in each row. Each stage total falls between 5 and 25.

| Stage | Statement numbers | Total |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Awareness** (seeing the belief clearly) | 1, 6, 11, 16, 21 | |
| **L. Leadership** (taking ownership of your story) | 2, 7, 12, 17, 22 | |
| **I. Identity** (rewriting who you believe you are) | 3, 8, 13, 18, 23 | |
| **G. Growth** (choosing expansion over comfort) | 4, 9, 14, 19, 24 | |
| **N. Navigation** (executing the path forward) | 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 | |
| **Overall total** | all 25 | |

The overall total is background, not the headline. The useful information is the
shape: which stage sits lowest, which sits highest, and how far apart they are.

## What the stage scores mean

### Awareness, 5 to 25

- **5 to 10.** You can feel that something is off but you cannot name it yet.
  That is a normal place to start and it is exactly what session one is for.
- **11 to 15.** You have a general sense of the pattern. The specific belief
  underneath it is still out of focus.
- **16 to 20.** You can name the pattern and usually catch it in motion. The
  work now is why it is still running.
- **21 to 25.** You see it clearly. Check that this is real sight and not a
  well-practiced explanation. Clarity that has not changed anything is often a
  story about yourself that you have simply told many times.

### Leadership, 5 to 25

- **5 to 10.** Most of your explanation for where you are sits outside you.
  Some of that is fair. The part that is not is where your power is.
- **11 to 15.** You own your part in some areas and hand it off in others.
  Notice which areas get the ownership and which get the excuse.
- **16 to 20.** You take responsibility reliably. The next step is acting on it
  faster, closer to the moment.
- **21 to 25.** You own your story. Watch that ownership has not turned into
  carrying everything alone, which is a different problem.

### Identity, 5 to 25

- **5 to 10.** There is real distance between who you say you are and how you
  operate. This is usually the most uncomfortable score and the most useful one.
- **11 to 15.** You know what you value. Your commitments contradict it more
  often than you would like.
- **16 to 20.** You mostly live from your own definition of yourself, with a few
  old labels still attached.
- **21 to 25.** Your self-description and your life agree. Keep testing it under
  pressure, because that is where old identities reappear.

### Growth, 5 to 25

- **5 to 10.** Comfort is winning. Not through laziness, usually through a full
  calendar that leaves no room for anything new.
- **11 to 15.** You grow when circumstances force it. Chosen growth is rarer.
- **16 to 20.** You take on hard things on purpose and stay with them past the
  first setback.
- **21 to 25.** You choose expansion routinely. Check that the direction is one
  you picked rather than one you inherited.

### Navigation, 5 to 25

- **5 to 10.** Intentions are not reaching your week. You need a system, not more
  willpower.
- **11 to 15.** You have a plan. It survives good weeks and not bad ones.
- **16 to 20.** You execute consistently and recover from most misses.
- **21 to 25.** You have a working system with review built into it. The risk
  here is executing well on the wrong target, so check it against your Awareness
  and Identity scores.

### Reading the shape

- **A low stage early in the sequence pulls everything after it down.** Low
  Awareness with high Navigation usually means efficient movement in a direction
  that was never chosen. Work the earlier stage first.
- **A gap of eight or more points between the highest and lowest stage** is the
  clearest signal of where the six weeks should focus.
- **Flat and low across all five** is common in people who are exhausted rather
  than lost. Start with Awareness and keep the first commitments very small.
- **Flat and high across all five** deserves a gentle second look. Either this
  person is genuinely aligned and here for a specific goal, or they answered the
  way they would want to be seen. Session one will show you which.

## Coach's guide

### Debriefing the assessment in session one

Put the debrief early, in the first ten to twelve minutes. It gives the client a
starting point that is data rather than complaint, and it sets the session goal
without you having to choose it for them.

Run it in this order.

1. **Read the five stage totals out loud.** Nothing else. No interpretation yet.
2. **Ask what they notice.** Let the client speak first. Their read on their own
   numbers is more useful than yours.
3. **Ask what surprised them, and what they already knew.** Those are two
   different conversations and both matter.
4. **Name the lowest stage and the highest stage.** Ask what they make of the
   distance between them.
5. **Set the session goal from what surfaces.** Usually a version of: see the
   gap clearly and name the belief that keeps it open.

Four things to avoid:

- **Do not diagnose.** Say what the scores show, not what they mean about the
  person. "Navigation came in lowest" is a fact. "You struggle with discipline"
  is a verdict you were not asked to hand down.
- **Do not reassure the low scores away.** A low score the client feels is more
  useful than a low score you softened. Stay steady rather than comforting.
- **Do not treat it as precise.** Call it what it is: a snapshot of how they saw
  themselves on one day, useful mostly for what it points at.
- **Do not let the debrief take the session.** Twelve minutes, then move into the
  exploration.

If a client protests that a statement was hard to score, that friction is worth
one question: "What made that one hard?" The answer is often the session.

### Comparing session one to session six

Retake the assessment live in session six, in the first ten minutes, before the
review conversation. Do not show them their old scores until the new ones are
written down.

Then work through it this way.

1. **Fill in the comparison table together.** Both score sets, the change, and a
   few words on what actually changed.
2. **Start with the largest movement.** Ask what caused it. Make them say the
   cause out loud, in behavior, not in feeling. "I did the review every Sunday"
   is a cause. "I feel better" is a result.
3. **Go to the flat or lower stage next.** Ask for their honest read. A stage
   that did not move is not a failure of the program. It is usually where the
   next ninety days live.
4. **Handle a drop directly.** Scores sometimes fall, most often in Awareness
   and Identity, because the client is now measuring against a more honest
   standard than they used six weeks ago. Say that plainly. A drop that comes
   from clearer sight is progress, and the client will usually recognize it the
   moment you name it.
5. **Point at the pattern, not the total.** The number that matters is the shape
   of the five, and whether the earlier stages have come up enough to support the
   later ones.
6. **Carry the result into the forward plan.** The lowest remaining stage sets
   the focus of the ninety-day plan on the closing pages of the workbook.

Give the client both score sets to keep. The comparison is theirs, and it is the
clearest proof of movement they will have.
